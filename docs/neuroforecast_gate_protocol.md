**NeuroForecast validation gate (v1.1): what is locked before the reserve is scored.** Written September 21, 2026 IST; amended the same day, before any reserve scoring, as recorded in the amendment section at the end. The machine-readable version is [experiments/neuroforecast_gate_protocol.json](../experiments/neuroforecast_gate_protocol.json); its SHA-256 is recorded in every gate manifest and in the development lock. The implementation is [tools/neuroforecast_gate.py](../tools/neuroforecast_gate.py), which imports the three frozen cores unchanged and never rewrites the completed `neuroforecast_selection_v1` outputs.

The gate answers the four questions in [the project brief](neuroforecast_project.md): is the target trustworthy, what is the warning decision, how reliable is a forecast, and does any of it hold on batches and chemicals the method has never seen.

**What stays the same.** The cohort is exactly the frozen 819 development cases, 101 identities and 12 dates; the gate's feature matrix reproduces the frozen `development_data.npz` with zero difference. The target, identity rules, exclusions, the leave-one-date-out split with purged identities, the primary date-macro MAE, and every hyperparameter are unchanged. The three reserved dates (20170920, 20171004, 20171011) and their 32 eligible identities remain absent from development. Dropping the day-5 requirement was checked and changes nothing (both rules give 819 cases), so day 5 stays in the cohort rule because the activity/coordination comparator uses it.

**What is new, and why.**

| Component | Rule | Why it is there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day-7 reliability indicators | Seven day-7 quantities: replicate count, replicate SD of the log2 control-relative network-spike value, and five plate-control statistics (count, arcsinh median, zero fraction, IQR/(median+1), silent fraction). Each case's three replicates sit on three plates, so plate statistics are averaged over the case's plates. | The brief asks for measurement reliability that a live rule can use at day 7. These are also the inputs of the difficulty model below. |
| One new forecasting candidate | `extra_trees_relative_day7_reliability` = the selected day-7 feature set plus the seven indicators. No further candidate may be added after the protocol hash. | Tests the specific hypothesis that day-7 replicate/control reliability carries information about day-12 outcomes. |
| Product-model rule | The learned candidate with the lowest full-development date-macro MAE in this run. Fixed before the reserve; never changed by reserve results. | Removes the temptation to pick a model after seeing the reserve. The tool always shows the forecast beside the activity/coordination comparator and persistence. |
| Nested selection estimate | Inside each outer training set, an inner purged leave-one-date-out over the 11 training dates scores every candidate; the inner winner's error on the outer date is recorded. | The 0.937 in the selection results is a post-selection number. The nested estimate is the honest one for the report. |
| Warning decision | Within each date, rank cases by a score and flag the top 20% (also 10% and 30%). Scores: product forecast magnitude, comparator forecast magnitude, and persistence (day-7 magnitude); random is the analytic floor. Reported for all cases and within the early-quiet subgroup. Primary metric: pooled recall of later large changes among early-quiet cases at the 20% budget. | A budget-ranking policy matches how a lab reviews a batch and avoids relying on uncalibrated magnitudes. The early-quiet subgroup is where a forecast can add anything beyond "look at what already changed". The 20% budget is fixed here, before any curve is computed. |
| Reliability | Split-conformal intervals around the product forecast at nominal 80% (primary) and 90%. Calibration residuals come from the inner purged loop, so each residual is from a model that never saw that case's date or chemical. A scaled variant uses a difficulty model sigma(x) fit on absolute residuals with out-of-fold sigma values for calibration. | The brief rejects nominal coverage without observed coverage. Observed coverage per date is reported, which is the honest test under batch and chemical shift. |
| Abstention | Cases whose sigma exceeds the development 80th percentile are marked "unreliable forecast". Retained fraction, retained/abstained MAE and retained coverage are reported. | Gives the tool a defensible way to say "do not trust this one". |
| Measurement-quality display | Four fixed flags: control median network spikes at day 7 below 1; fewer than 4 controls; replicate SD or control IQR ratio above the development 90th percentile. Reported with MAE on flagged versus unflagged cases. No pass/fail. | Display and reporting only. Development data show only moderate association between day-7 and day-12 control activity (Spearman 0.42 across 77 plates), so no day-7 flag claims to predict later control failure. |
| Outcome-quality sensitivity subset | Cases whose day-12 plate control median is at least 5 network spikes (42 development cases fall below). | Uses a future observation, so it is a reporting subset for target reliability, never a feature or decision. |

**Reserve scoring: once.** The `reserve` stage refuses to run without the development lock, unchanged hashes of the protocol, gate code and frozen cores, an explicit `--unseal` flag, and the absence of earlier reserve outputs. It fits every candidate on the 819 development cases, calibrates the intervals on the full-development out-of-fold residuals, predicts every eligible reserve case, and writes case-level predictions, intervals, flags, per-date and pooled metrics, and the criteria below. Every reserve date is reported whatever its result. A rerun requires a new protocol version and a written explanation.

**Criteria and what each outcome means for the entry (v1.1).**

| Criterion | Pass rule (fixed now) | If it passes | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1a forecast vs simple baselines | Product mean-of-three-dates MAE below persistence and the control reference, and below persistence on at least two of three dates. | Claim lower forecast error than carrying day 7 forward or assuming no change, on three later, chemically disjoint batches, with the magnitude and n = 3 stated. | State that the forecast did not beat trivial rules on the reserve; the tool's value rests on the reliability layer alone. |
| C1b forecast vs small model | Product mean MAE below the activity/coordination comparator, and below it on at least two of three dates. | Claim that the day-7 control-relative representation beats the smaller model on the reserve. | No advantage claim over the smaller model; report which was better; keep both in the tool. |
| C2a warning vs naive rule | At the 20% budget among early-quiet cases, pooled over the reserve, product recall exceeds persistence-rank recall and the random expectation. | Claim that the forecast prioritizes follow-up better than ranking by what already changed, with the counts. | No warning-advantage claim; the ranking is presented as a comparison tool. |
| C2b warning vs small model | Same budget and subgroup: product recall at least the comparator's. | State that the product forecast is no worse than the smaller model for prioritization. | State that the smaller model detected more; keep both policies visible. |
| C3 reliability | Pooled observed coverage at nominal 80% is at least 0.70 for the scaled intervals, and retained-case MAE is below all-case MAE under the locked abstention threshold. | Claim intervals with the stated observed coverage under shift, and a usable unreliable-forecast flag. | Intervals shown as development-calibrated only, with the observed shortfall printed; no abstention claim. |
| C4 display | None; descriptive. | | |

No criterion may be altered, no case excluded, and no comparator weakened after reserve outcomes are visible. A failed criterion narrows the claim; it does not trigger a second reserve evaluation. Nothing in this gate supports living-chip or clinical validity, toxicity or recovery claims, saved days or wells, a new learning algorithm, or broad external validity: the reserve is three later batches from one public study.

**Amendment v1.1, September 21, 2026 IST, before any reserve scoring.** The v1 development run (lock SHA-256 `d96474db59eed33e690f428bcb76db1a51e5aa288e0da96697ced8cfc65d8177`, outputs kept in `neuroforecast_gate_v1`) showed that the product-versus-small-model difference is within selection noise (nested estimate 1.133) and that the two learned warning policies detected 22 and 23 of 45 early-quiet later changes at the 20% budget. The original C1 and C2 bundled the claim against trivial baselines with the claim against the team's own smaller model, so a comparator-driven FAIL would have hidden a real result. C1 and C2 were therefore split into the a/b pairs above. Every comparison is still computed and reported; the cohort, target, inputs, candidates, product-model rule, budgets, nominal levels, locked thresholds, reliability method, flags, sensitivity subset, reserve procedure and C3 are unchanged. This amendment was informed by development results and precedes the reserve; it is disclosed as such.

The first v1.1 re-run also exposed a reproducibility defect: forecasts reproduced to 3.6e-15, but the difficulty model's absolute-residual targets carried floating-point summation-order noise from multi-threaded tree prediction, which moved sigma by up to 0.069 and the abstention threshold from 1.5445 to 1.5433 while retained MAE (0.676 versus 0.675) and coverage (0.807 versus 0.811) were essentially unchanged. The gate now predicts single-threaded (identical trees and seeds, fixed summation order) and rounds absolute residuals to 1e-9 before fitting the difficulty model; the frozen cores and hyperparameters are untouched. That run is kept as `neuroforecast_gate_v1_1_nondeterministic_sigma`. The final v1.1 development stage was run twice into separate directories to demonstrate identical output hashes before the reserve was unsealed.
