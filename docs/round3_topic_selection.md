**Working topic selected: NeuroForecast, an early warning tool for later changes in neuronal function.** The public-data selection sprint completed on September 20, 2026 IST. Advance this problem to a bounded validation gate. The current model is an exploratory prototype; its advantage over the strongest small baseline remains uncertain.

The question is concrete: **given day-7 electrical measurements from chemical-exposed neuronal cultures and their matched controls, can we flag substantial changes in the day-12 network-spike readout for chemicals and experiment batches absent from training?** A researcher could use a reliable warning to prioritize follow-up measurements or inspection. Actual savings, safe assay termination, and improved biological decisions still require evidence.

This decision closes the three-dataset search authorized in [the selection protocol](round3_selection_protocol.md). The deliverables are verified source inventories, a reproducible baseline comparison, a prior-work assessment, and an executable example. The earlier DosePilot and OxyGuard gate failures are preserved. No public release, finished interface, or competition submission has been produced.

**The quantitative signal supports another gate, rather than a winning-method claim.** The first frozen model improved mean error only 5.1% over its best simple comparator, with substantial uncertainty. One documented follow-up compared control-relative representations using the same cases and splits. The best variant used day 7 alone, reaching a mean absolute error of 0.937 in the log2 relative readout, compared with 1.105 for persistence and 0.973 for a smaller activity/coordination model. That is a 15.2% reduction versus persistence and a 3.7% reduction versus the stronger small model. Both paired-date uncertainty intervals include no improvement.

In the predefined subgroup with little day-7 change, the selected model detects 24 of 45 later large changes, with 13 false alerts. The smaller model detects 23 with eight false alerts. Thus, the day-7 model has the best aggregate forecast error among tested variants; it has not established the best warning policy. These are development comparisons on correlated dose-condition cases, not independent clinical outcomes.

Why advance this topic despite those limits? It offers a directly measured biological readout, a reproducible local experiment, a practical question, and a meaningful reserved validation cohort. The central uncertainty can be tested with existing public data. Continuing broad topic searches would consume the time needed to establish whether this workflow improves a decision. The next phase should resolve that question before substantial interface work.

**Prior work limits the novelty claim.** The search included six archived Europe PMC queries, targeted data-record searches, source papers, and the existing research cache. It was bounded through September 19, 2026 and is not an exhaustive proof of novelty.

Other participants can discover these public datasets and topics. Kaggle Writeups are hidden until closure, so actual topic overlap cannot be determined. A credible contribution would come from a useful, independently evaluated decision and transparent reproduction. Extra Trees, control normalization, and forecasting are established techniques.

**The next gate is validation of the warning decision.** Preserve the three reserved dates, 20170920, 20171004 and 20171011, until the method and evaluation rules are locked. Their 32 eligible substance identities are absent from development, including earlier repeats. Develop the control/replicate reliability assessment and compare warning policies with the strong small baseline using only development data. Fix the operating point and failure criteria before scoring the reserve once. If useful performance does not survive that test, narrow the product claim rather than reporting exploratory gains as confirmed.

The selected source is rat cortical cultures on MEAs. It is relevant to functional monitoring of neuronal in-vitro systems, including a possible future neural-chip workflow, but it does not validate perfused organ chips, humans, or clinical toxicity. Public cell/toxicology data with a clear connection to organ-chip work can fit the competition's scope; this bridge should be explained explicitly in the eventual Tool & Platform submission.
